It is expected that currency depreciation will increase exports as it makes a country’s local or domestic goods cheaper for the foreign customers and makes the economy more competitive in the international market. However, it cannot always be the case as export growth is also dependent on global demand and the cost of raw materials that are imported.

The gdp growth, inflation, fdi and trade openness will help differentiate the country-years experiencing export contraction from those not experiencing that contraction. An increase in economic activity, foreign direct investment and trade can boost exports whereas high inflation can make goods less competitive. Though these effects can vary across countries.

We can expect the model to fail as it may not fully account for some of the export contractions as exports are highly affected by unexpected events and a range of other factors e.g. global crisis, political instability, or a sudden change in fdi. 
These events can be hard to predict, so it may be difficult to guess most of the years of contraction.
